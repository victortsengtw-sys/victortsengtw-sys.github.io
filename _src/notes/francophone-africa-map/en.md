Mention "francophone Africa" and most people think only of Côte d'Ivoire (in West Africa, French-speaking, the world's largest cocoa producer) or Senegal. In fact, it stretches from West Africa through Central Africa to the Indian Ocean. According to the 2026 report of the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF), there are about 396 million French speakers worldwide, 65% of whom live in Africa. But "French-speaking country" has no single definition: whether French is an official language and how many people actually speak it are two different questions.

## The whole picture in one table

The shares of French speakers below (native and second-language speakers combined) are OIF estimates for 2022.

| Region | Countries | Status of French | Share of French speakers |
|---|---|---|---|
| West Africa | Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal, Guinea, Benin, Togo | Official language | 26%–41% |
| West Africa (Sahel) | Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger | Recently redesignated a "working language" | 13%–24% |
| Central Africa | Gabon, Republic of the Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo | Official language | 51%–65% |
| Central Africa | Cameroon (alongside English), Chad (alongside Arabic), Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea (alongside Spanish and Portuguese) | One of the official languages | 13%–41% |
| East Africa and Indian Ocean | Djibouti (with Arabic), Burundi, Rwanda, Comoros, Madagascar, Seychelles | Co-official with other languages | 6%–53% |
| North Africa and others | Algeria, Morocco, Tunisia, Mauritania, Mauritius | Not official, but widely used | e.g. Mauritius 73%, Mauritania 13% |

A few figures worth noting: about 34% of Côte d'Ivoire's population speaks French, and about 65% of Gabon's. The Democratic Republic of the Congo, with its large population, has around 49 million French speakers, more than any other African country. Equatorial Guinea only made French an official language in 1997.

## Changes in three Sahel countries

In recent years, three West African countries have changed the legal status of French:

- **Mali**: a new constitution approved by referendum in July 2023 makes 13 national languages official, with French becoming a "working language".
- **Burkina Faso**: a constitutional amendment passed by the transitional legislative assembly on 30 December 2023 and promulgated on 22 January 2024 made national languages official, with French and English both listed as working languages.
- **Niger**: a charter promulgated at the end of March 2025 designates Hausa as the national language, with French and English as working languages.

"Working language" does not mean French has stopped being used, however. In Mali's case, researchers note that the constitution does not define "working language", an arrangement that allows French to remain in administrative use during a transition period. When dealing with these countries, check the latest local announcements for the language actually required in documents.

## "French-speaking country" does not mean "everyone speaks French"

As the table shows, even where French is an official language, the share of the population that speaks it is mostly between 30% and 60%. Many countries also recognise several national languages (langues nationales), and the languages of everyday life are far more complex than any table of official languages. For businesses, French is enough for government, banks and most business settings, but in consumer markets the role of local languages still has to be taken into account.

## One line for Asian companies

Treat francophone Africa as a language sphere rather than a single market: French opens the door to more than twenty countries, but behind each door the languages and habits are different.
