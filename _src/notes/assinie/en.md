In Côte d'Ivoire (in West Africa, French-speaking, the world's largest cocoa producer), when people in Abidjan talk about "a weekend at the seaside", they very often mean Assinie. With the Atlantic on one side and a calm lagoon on the other, it is a place where resorts, villas and fishing villages sit side by side. If you work in Abidjan or are there on business, it is one of the easiest short breaks to arrange.

## A narrow peninsula between lagoon and ocean

Assinie-Mafia lies in the south-east of Côte d'Ivoire on the Gulf of Guinea. Administratively it belongs to the Adiaké department, in the Sud-Comoé region of the Comoé district. Sources put it roughly 80 to 94 km from Abidjan; it is an easy drive, and the road ends in the town itself.

The town stretches along both sides of the Aby Lagoon's outlet to the sea. Opposite lies a narrow peninsula about 15 km long, with the lagoon on one side and the ocean on the other, dotted with villas, holiday bungalows and upmarket hotels. You can reach it by car, by private boat or by crossing the lagoon in a pirogue (dugout canoe). At the tip of the peninsula is La Passe, where lagoon meets ocean: an open, sweeping view and a popular spot for a stroll.

## A trading post older than Abidjan

Assinie's history goes back further than most people imagine. It was one of the earliest trading posts on the Ivorian coast. Capuchin missionaries arrived as early as 1637 but left because of the climate and disease; in 1687 French missionaries and merchants settled here, and between 1701 and 1704 the French built Fort Saint-Louis. Two young men from Assinie, Aniaba and Banga, were taken to France at the time. Aniaba was baptised there, later returned to West Africa and, in 1704, became adviser to the king of Quita, in what is today the Togo area.

In 1907 Assinie-Mafia was still an important port, before it was gradually overtaken by Grand-Bassam, Bingerville and Abidjan. The 1978 French comedy *Les Bronzés* was also filmed here.

According to the 2014 census, the sous-préfecture of Assinie-Mafia had about 16,700 inhabitants, including some 5,700 in the village of Assinie-Mafia and about 1,700 in Assinie-France. Reports from 2025 estimate that the local population now exceeds 22,000, most of whom make their living from fishing.

Tourism is a priority sector for the Ivorian government. According to 2025 reports, tourism accounted for about 7% of GDP in 2023, and the government aims to raise this to 10%. Assinie is one of the flagships of coastal tourism.

## What to do in Assinie

| Type | What's on offer |
|---|---|
| Beaches | Sandy shoreline, attracting more and more surfers in recent years |
| Lagoon activities | Some upmarket hotels offer jet skis, canoe trips on the lagoon, towed inflatables and short boat cruises |
| Walks and views | La Passe, at the tip of the peninsula |
| Local life | Fishing villages and everyday scenes along the lagoon |

Accommodation ranges from upmarket resorts to guesthouses (maisons d'hôtes). Weekends and public holidays are peak time for Abidjan day-trippers, so if you want to stay on the peninsula, confirm the boat transfer and check-in arrangements in advance. Before going into the sea, it is also worth asking your hotel about the day's sea conditions.

## One line for Asian readers

Think of Assinie as Abidjan's "back-garden coast": in a day or two you can enjoy the lagoon and the beach and touch on a chapter of early colonial trading history, which makes it ideal for the weekend of a business trip.
