Côte d'Ivoire (in West Africa, French-speaking, the world's largest cocoa producer) does not produce many films, but several of its works have left their mark at Cannes, Venice and FESPACO, Africa's major film festival. Getting to know these directors and films is a shortcut to understanding Ivorian society and its sense of humour, and a common topic of conversation in cultural exchanges and co-productions.

## The 1980s: social observation through comedy

**Désiré Écaré, *Visages de femmes* (1985)**: this comedy-drama was selected for International Critics' Week at the 1985 Cannes Film Festival and won the FIPRESCI (International Federation of Film Critics) Prize. Written and directed by Écaré and produced through Films de la Lagune, it was released in France on 26 June 1985. Albertine N'Guessan plays a woman running a smoked-fish business in a seaside town, struggling to support her family and two daughters.

**Henri Duparc, *Bal poussière* (released 1989)**: Duparc, who studied film in Belgrade, uses a humorous, unconventional approach to tell the story of a farm owner calling himself a "demi-god" who wants to take another wife — Binta, a young woman from Abidjan — while Binta longs to return to the city and the man she loves. The film touches on traditional customs, the urban-rural divide and women's rights. In 2021, a restored version was screened in the Cannes Classics section of the Cannes Film Festival, a project led jointly by France's Centre national du cinéma (CNC) and the Henri Duparc Foundation: the CNC's laboratory restored the original 16mm negative and scanned it in 2K, while L'Image Retrouvée restored the sound from the original 16mm magnetic tape.

## Two Golden Stallions of Yennenga at FESPACO

At FESPACO (the Pan-African Film Festival), held in Burkina Faso, the top prize is the Golden Stallion of Yennenga (Étalon d'or de Yennenga). According to 2023 reports, Ivorian directors have won it twice:

| Year | Director | Film |
|---|---|---|
| 1981 | Kramo-Lanciné Fadika | *Djéli, conte d'aujourd'hui* |
| 1993 | Roger Gnoan M'Bala | *Au nom du Christ* |

Roger Gnoan M'Bala was born in Grand-Bassam in 1943 and studied film in Sweden and Paris. His first work, *Koundoum* (1970), was a black-and-white documentary about traditional dance. *Au nom du Christ*, a satire on religious syncretism, also won a youth award at the Locarno Film Festival in Switzerland; his 2000 film *Adanggaman* won the Special Jury Prize at the 2001 Marrakech International Film Festival. He died in Abidjan in 2023, aged 82.

## A new generation: Philippe Lacôte

Philippe Lacôte, who grew up in Abidjan, saw his first feature, *Run*, selected for Un Certain Regard at Cannes in 2014. His second feature, *La Nuit des rois* (English title *Night of the Kings*), premiered in the Orizzonti section of the Venice Film Festival in September 2020. It tells the story of a young man's first night in La Maca prison; it was chosen as Côte d'Ivoire's submission for the International Feature Film award at the 2021 Oscars and distributed in the United States by NEON.

It is worth noting that *Black and White in Color*, Côte d'Ivoire's 1976 Oscar submission, went on to win Best Foreign Language Film.

## Local festivals and industry events

- **Clap Ivoire**: a short-film competition organised by Côte d'Ivoire's national cinema office (ONAC-CI), bringing together young directors from the eight member states of the West African Economic and Monetary Union (UEMOA) with 13-minute fiction and documentary films. The 25th edition took place in 2025.
- **SICA (Abidjan's international film and audiovisual fair)**: the 3rd edition was held in June 2025 and included a pitch competition; the next edition is scheduled for 25–27 November 2026.

## One line for Asian readers

To get started, look for the restored *Bal poussière* and *La Nuit des rois*; if you work in film or television, SICA and Clap Ivoire are practical ways to connect with Ivorian creators.
