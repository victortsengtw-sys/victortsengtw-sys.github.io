If you do business in Côte d'Ivoire (in West Africa, French-speaking, the world's largest cocoa producer), tax is an unavoidable part of your cost estimates. This article sets out the main taxes and the authorities responsible, so you can keep up when you talk to a local accountant. It is an overview only: rates and thresholds can change, so always rely on official announcements and professional advice for your actual situation.

## Who administers tax

- **Directorate General of Taxes (Direction Générale des Impôts, DGI)**: responsible for domestic taxes such as corporate income tax and VAT.
- **Directorate General of Customs (Direction Générale des Douanes, DGD)**: responsible for import duties and related charges.

The DGI runs e-impôts, an online platform for filing and paying taxes. According to official information, it is currently open to companies under the Large Enterprises Directorate (DGE) and the Medium-sized Enterprises Centres (CME); a taxpayer number (NCC) must be obtained before joining.

## Main taxes at a glance

| Tax | Key points |
|---|---|
| Corporate income tax | 25% in general; 30% for telecoms and ICT operators |
| Minimum tax (IMF) | 0.5% of turnover, with an amount between XOF 3 million and XOF 35 million; lower rates for petroleum products, water and electricity, and the financial sector; newly created companies are exempt in their first financial year |
| VAT (TVA) | Standard rate 18%; a reduced 9% rate applies to petroleum products, solar equipment and certain foods (such as milk, infant formula and durum wheat pasta); exports are zero-rated |
| VAT-exempt items | Include books and newspapers, medicines, unprocessed foods and educational services |
| Employer payroll tax | 2.8% of salaries for local employees, 12% for expatriate employees |
| Non-resident withholding | Foreign companies without a permanent establishment are generally subject to 20% withholding on local income |

Corporate income tax, the minimum tax and turnover thresholds are based mainly on the July 2025 summary by Germany Trade & Invest (GTAI); VAT details and payroll tax are based on Lloyds Bank market information updated in August 2026.

## Tax regimes by turnover

Côte d'Ivoire assigns businesses to different regimes according to annual turnover (GTAI, 2025):

- **Standard actual regime (réel normal)**: XOF 500 million and above.
- **Simplified actual regime (réel simplifié)**: XOF 200 million to 500 million; same rates, but simpler filing.
- **Micro-enterprise regime**: XOF 50 million to 200 million, taxed at 6% of turnover.
- **Small entrepreneur regime (régime de l'entreprenant)**: XOF 5 million to 50 million, taxed at 5% of turnover, or 4% for trading activities.

Businesses must register for VAT only once turnover exceeds XOF 200 million. Foreign service providers may appoint a local representative; otherwise, the local buyer pays the VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism.

## Going digital: online filing and e-invoicing

Tax digitalisation has been a priority in recent years. An IMF report in 2026 listed the rollout of e-invoicing as one of the measures to broaden the tax base. According to a summary by e-invoicing provider Edicom, the DGI's certified electronic invoicing system became mandatory in phases from December 2025 for businesses under the standard regime, the micro-enterprise regime and the small entrepreneur regime; invoices must first be validated by the DGI and receive an electronic tax stamp.

## One line for Asian companies

First work out which tax regime your company will fall under, then ask a local accountant to estimate your tax burden based on the latest finance law, and confirm early how you will connect to the e-invoicing system.
