On the southwest coast of Côte d'Ivoire (in West Africa, French-speaking, the world's largest cocoa producer) lies the quiet little town of Sassandra. Once a major player in the country's foreign trade, it now draws travellers with its colourful fishing boats, its bay and its old buildings. Compared with lively Assinie, it suits those who want to take their time watching the sea and reading history.

## From San-Andréa to colonial port

Sassandra sits at the mouth of the river of the same name, more than 250 km from Abidjan; about 65 km to the southwest is San-Pédro. Portuguese navigators had already reached this estuary in the 15th century and called it San-Andréa.

In the colonial era, Sassandra was an administrative and commercial centre: from 1906 to 1912 it was the seat of the administrative capital. In the 1950s, its metal pier (the Wharf) jutting out into the sea was one of the country's main gateways for foreign trade. Later, with the opening of the Vridi Canal and the rise of the port of Abidjan, Sassandra's role as a port faded.

## A fishing port and its colourful fleet

Walking along Sassandra's bay today, what catches the eye are the colourful wooden boats of the Fanti fishermen. In 2020, through cooperation between the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA) and the Ivorian government, a new fish landing quay and central market opened; the central market has about 900 traders. Total investment in the two facilities was about 18.2 billion CFA francs, of which the Ivorian government contributed about 2.6 billion.

| Year | Fish landed (tonnes) |
|---|---|
| 2019 | approx. 5,513 |
| 2020 | approx. 4,841 |

A 2022 report noted that the authorities expected annual output to reach about 8,000 tonnes from 2025.

## Places worth seeing

| Site | Highlights |
|---|---|
| The old Wharf | Rusting metal structure, considered one of the few such facilities still visible in West Africa |
| Governor's House (Maison du gouverneur) | Built in 1895, overlooking the estuary; now a ruin |
| Lighthouse | Built before the Second World War |
| Colonial-era buildings | Old prison, French treasury office, early bank and gendarmerie buildings |
| SS Dumana memorial | Commemorates a British supply ship sunk offshore in December 1943 |
| Beaches and the Fresco cliffs | The beaches are popular with campers; to the east, around Fresco, stretch long sea cliffs |

The town also has a regional school founded in 1899 and the century-old, Gothic-style Saint-André Catholic church. The story behind the SS Dumana memorial: on the evening of 24 December 1943, this British supply ship was hit by a German submarine off Sassandra; of the 169 people on board, about 40 died. The memorial was unveiled the following December.

## Further afield

If you have an extra day, head out of town. About 7 km from the centre, Grand-Drewin is home to the former residence of Captain Alfred Émile Schiffer, who between 1910 and 1912 founded what is regarded as the country's first industrial oil mill there. Grand-Drewin also has a semi-underground cave which, according to local oral tradition, is linked to a point of embarkation from the era of the slave trade. Inland, Gaoulou has the General Weygand Bridge, built in 1940.

Every year at the end of July, the Sêkêh cultural festival is held in Sassandra and Fresco. Note that most of the old buildings have long been neglected and can mostly be viewed only from the outside; a 2026 report also called on the government to draw up a restoration plan.

## One line for Asian readers

Sassandra fits well into a trip to San-Pédro or along the southwest coast: take in the daily rhythm of the fishing port, and read Côte d'Ivoire's early trading history in the old Wharf and the Governor's House.
