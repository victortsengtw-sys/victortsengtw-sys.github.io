When you work with companies, associations or public bodies in Côte d'Ivoire (in West Africa, French-speaking, the world's largest cocoa producer), formal meetings are almost always held in French, and the documents before and after are in French too. Understanding the names and wording of these documents tells you which ones are mere memos and which carry formal weight.

## Before the meeting: notice and agenda

| French | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| avis de convocation | Notice of meeting | Usually accompanied by the agenda; sending it as an email attachment is accepted |
| projet d'ordre du jour | Draft agenda | Until it is adopted at the start of the meeting, the agenda is only a draft |
| ordre du jour | Agenda | Lists the items to be discussed at the meeting |
| séance ordinaire / extraordinaire | Regular / special meeting | The agenda of a special meeting cannot be changed |
| questions diverses | Any other business | New items can be added here at a regular meeting |

A typical agenda runs roughly as follows: opening of the meeting (ouverture de la séance), adoption of the agenda, approval of the minutes of the previous meeting, discussion item by item, any other business, date and place of the next meeting, and close of the meeting (levée / clôture de la séance).

## During the meeting: verbs you will hear

- **adopter l'ordre du jour**: to adopt the agenda. Note that the agenda is *adopté*, while minutes are *approuvé*; the two verbs are not interchangeable.
- **approuver le procès-verbal**: to approve the minutes (of the previous meeting).
- **le quorum**: the quorum; where the by-laws require one, check first that it has been reached.
- **Il est proposé que… / Il est décidé que…**: "It is proposed that… / It is resolved that…", the set phrases used to record motions and decisions in formal minutes.
- **à l'unanimité / à la majorité**: unanimously / by a majority.

## After the meeting: compte rendu vs procès-verbal

English calls both "minutes", but French distinguishes two documents:

| | compte rendu (c. r.) | procès-verbal (PV, P.-V.) |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Internal memo summarising discussions and decisions | Official record, often required by the rules |
| Format | More flexible; bullet points or telegraphic style are fine | Fixed format, following the agenda item by item |
| Content | Place, date, attendees, agenda, key points per item, closing time, author | Also includes lists of those present and absent, quorum, chair and secretary, mover and seconder, voting results |
| Follow-up | No need to approve it at the next meeting | Usually approved at the next meeting and signed by the chair and/or secretary |

Formal minutes are written in the present tense, impersonally, in an objective and neutral tone. The Office québécois de la langue française (OQLF) also points out that *minutes* and *varia* are discouraged usages, to be replaced by procès-verbal and questions diverses, and that *dresser un procès-verbal* refers to an official act such as issuing a ticket, not to taking minutes of a meeting.

These usages are drawn from Quebec and Canadian federal French-language standards, and institutions elsewhere may have slightly different habits; for formal documents, follow any template the other party provides.

## Useful phrases for chairing or attending a meeting

- *Je déclare la séance ouverte.*: I declare the meeting open.
- *Avez-vous des modifications à apporter à l'ordre du jour ?*: Are there any changes to the agenda?
- *Je propose d'ajouter ce point aux questions diverses.*: I propose adding this item under any other business.
- *Pourriez-vous m'envoyer le compte rendu de la réunion ?*: Could you send me the summary of the meeting?
- *La séance est levée.*: The meeting is closed.

If you are representing the foreign party, confirming after the meeting who will write the minutes, when they will be sent and who will approve them makes follow-up much clearer.

## One line for Asian teams

Check any procès-verbal you receive carefully, as it may become the formal reference later on; a compte rendu, on the other hand, is a good tool for confirming mutual understanding, and sending one to the other side on your own initiative earns you credit.
