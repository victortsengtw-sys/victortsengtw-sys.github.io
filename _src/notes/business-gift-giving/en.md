In Côte d'Ivoire (in West Africa, French-speaking, the world's largest cocoa producer), a well-chosen small gift is often more memorable than a presentation. In its 2024 overview of local business culture, *Forbes Afrique* noted that Ivorians place great value on the thought behind a gift, and that gifts from foreign partners tend to be especially appreciated. What matters is not the price but the timing and a sense of proportion.

## When to give

There are three common occasions for giving gifts:

- **When a deal is concluded or a contract signed**: *Forbes Afrique* notes that gifts are especially meaningful when sealing a partnership and at important moments of hosting and welcome.
- **When invited to someone's home or to an event**: several business-culture guides advise that if you are invited to someone's home or a private gathering, bringing a small gift is basic courtesy.
- **When visiting a village or traditional chief**: rural visits usually have their own greeting protocol and customary gifts, so it is best to ask a local guide in advance (see our article "Visiting Villages and Traditional Chiefs").

A gift is not necessarily expected at a first formal meeting; if you do give one, choose something small and discreet.

## What to give

The common principle across all the advice is: **small and thoughtful, never extravagant**. An overly valuable gift may make the recipient uncomfortable or even be read as implying an expectation.

| Type | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Specialities from home | Tea, coffee or fine pastries from your home country | They carry a "from far away" story and make easy conversation starters |
| Company keepsakes | Good-quality notebooks, pens, small branded items | Suitable for a first partnership or a large team |
| Sweets | Chocolates, boxed treats | Ideal when visiting someone's home |

It is worth knowing that one of the classic gifts Ivorians give a host is a traditional hand-woven pagne (cloth / wrapper). If you receive one, it means the giver values the relationship highly.

## Religious and dietary considerations

Côte d'Ivoire has large Muslim and Christian populations; a 2025 guide by the French volunteer organisation France Volontaires cites figures of about 42% Muslim and 34% Christian. So unless you are sure of the recipient's habits, **do not give alcohol as a business gift**, and avoid food containing pork or alcohol. When in doubt, tea and non-food keepsakes are the safest choice.

## How to give and receive

- **Hand gifts over with your right hand or both hands**: locally, giving or receiving something with the left hand is generally considered impolite.
- **Briefly explain where the gift comes from**: a line such as "this tea comes from the mountains back home" conveys more than elaborate wrapping.
- **Show appreciation when you receive a gift**: when someone gives you something, likewise accept it with your right hand or both hands and say thank you.

## Compliance reminder

The line between a gift and a bribe is defined by each country's laws and by company policy. If the recipient is a public official or the gift is of significant value, check your company's compliance rules first and consult professional advisers or the relevant authorities; do not rely on "local custom" as your guide.

## One line for Asian teams

A box of tea from home with a sincere word of introduction is usually more appropriate than an expensive present; remember to hand it over with your right hand, and steer clear of alcohol.
