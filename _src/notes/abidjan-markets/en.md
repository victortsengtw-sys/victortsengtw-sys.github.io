In Côte d'Ivoire (in West Africa, French-speaking, the world's largest cocoa producer), markets are more than places to shop — they are the best window onto daily life, food and fashion. Each district of Abidjan has markets with their own speciality. Knowing "what is sold where" turns a market visit into efficient shopping or field research.

## Treichville: fabric, food and the tailors' quarter

The **Grand marché de Treichville** stands near the Treichville town hall. The old market was destroyed by fire in March 1997; the rebuilt market opened on 15 October 2003 and covers about 18,550 square metres over two floors. It sells a wide range of goods, including staple produce (vivriers), wax-print fabrics (pagnes), poultry and meat, and second-hand clothing, and traders are organised into different associations by product category.

Treichville also has several specialised markets, such as a small market known for fish, plantains and vegetables, and a two-storey dried-fish market; the latter has been described as a key source of the dried fish sold at Adjamé market.

If fashion interests you, Treichville's streets are home to many designers, stylists and tailoring workshops, with small shops nearby selling lace, embroidery and sewing supplies, and fabric traders form an indispensable link in the whole chain. Local media have called the area a "fashion crossroads", and the well-known brand Pathé'O also has ties to the neighbourhood. You can pick your fabric at the market first, then have a nearby tailor make it up to measure.

## CAVA: the handicraft hub

Also in Treichville, the **Centre artisanal de la ville d'Abidjan (CAVA)** opened in 1990 as a place where artisans make and sell their work on site. Here you can find wood carvings and small statues, ceramic plates, bronze pieces and the traditional West African board game awalé, among other things. The artisans come from several countries — there are bronze casters from Burkina Faso, for example. CAVA is a convenient starting point for choosing souvenirs or watching crafts being made; if you want to buy older pieces of heritage value, look separately into the relevant regulations and proof of provenance.

## Cocody: a rebuilt multi-storey market

The **Cocody Saint-Jean market** is the main commercial hub of the Cocody district. It has been rebuilt as a modern multi-storey building, replacing the former handicraft market. It offers a wide variety of goods and is handy for doing all your everyday shopping in one go.

## Adjamé: the food wholesale hub

Adjamé is Abidjan's long-distance bus hub. The district's **Gouro market** focuses on staple produce and is run by a cooperative; a reconstruction plan announced by the cooperative in 2021 aims to create a new market able to house more than 3,500 traders and supply markets across Abidjan more efficiently. For anyone working in food, agricultural produce or distribution research, it is a key stop for understanding how food flows through Abidjan.

| Market | Main features |
|---|---|
| Grand marché de Treichville | Fabric, staple produce, meat, second-hand clothing |
| Treichville tailors' quarter | Made-to-measure clothing, sewing supplies |
| CAVA | Wood carvings, bronze, ceramics and other crafts |
| Cocody Saint-Jean market | Multi-storey general market, everyday shopping |
| Gouro market, Adjamé | Staple produce, wholesale supply |

## One line for Asian readers

Rather than wandering Abidjan's markets aimlessly, decide first whether you are there for fabric, crafts or food; go with questions in mind, and the market will tell you consumer details you will never read in a report.
