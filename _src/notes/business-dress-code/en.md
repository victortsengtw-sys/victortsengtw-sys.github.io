In Côte d'Ivoire (in West Africa, French-speaking, the world's largest cocoa producer), what you wear is part of the first impression. A 2024 overview of business culture by *Forbes Afrique* put appearance at the top of its list of tips: being well dressed is seen as a mark of respect for the other person. Knowing when a suit is right and when a pagne is right will help you strike the right note both in the boardroom and at ceremonies.

## The boardroom: more formal than you might think

The US Department of Commerce's Country Commercial Guide, updated in 2026, notes that business customs in Côte d'Ivoire are generally more formal than in the United States; management at large companies mostly wear Western business attire, and women usually wear dresses or suits that fall below the knee.

Practical tips:

- **Choose tropical-weight suits**: Abidjan is hot and humid all year round, and the same guide recommends tropical-weight suits and clothing throughout the year.
- **Err on the formal side at first meetings**: for an initial meeting or a visit to a government office or large company, a jacket and shirt are the safest choice; once you know each other, adjust to how your counterparts dress.
- **Details get noticed**: *Forbes Afrique* also notes that details such as shoes, watch and phone are often used to gauge a person's standing; neatness matters more than designer labels.

## Pagne and wax: more than fabric

In Côte d'Ivoire, *pagne* broadly refers to cloth that can be wrapped or tailored into clothing, including brightly coloured wax prints and the various traditional hand-woven fabrics.

- **Where wax comes from**: a 2025 *Forbes Afrique* article explains that the name "wax" comes from the batik technique of Java, Indonesia; Dutch manufacturers began producing it mechanically in Helmond in 1846, and it then reached West Africa by ship. Today the related brands also have production sites in Abidjan.
- **The weight of hand-woven pagne**: traditional hand-woven pagne is often given to a host as a precious gift, and it also appears at important occasions such as weddings and baptisms.
- **Promoting local fabric**: in 2022, officials at the Ivorian Ministry of Commerce floated the idea of public-sector staff wearing clothes made of local fabric every Friday to support the textile industry; reports at the time said this was still a plan awaiting agreement from the parties involved.

For foreigners, wearing a shirt or dress made from pagne to a festival, wedding or cultural event is generally seen as a friendly gesture. For formal business meetings, however, Western business attire remains the norm unless your hosts explicitly invite otherwise.

## Religious sites and villages

| Occasion | Advice |
|---|---|
| Churches (e.g. the Basilica of Our Lady of Peace in Yamoussoukro) | Cover your shoulders; wear long trousers or a long skirt and closed shoes |
| Mosques | Avoid shorts; women may want to bring a headscarf in case they are asked to wear one |
| Village visits | Dress conservatively; avoid shorts and sleeveless tops |
| Anywhere | Do not wear military uniforms or anything resembling military or police clothing |

Travel advice from the Belgian Ministry of Foreign Affairs points out that wearing military or paramilitary clothing is prohibited throughout Côte d'Ivoire; it is best not to pack camouflage-style clothing.

## One line for Asian teams

Wear a lightweight suit for business and feel free to switch to a pagne shirt for celebrations; and remember, respect the occasion first, then express your personal style.
