Côte d'Ivoire (in West Africa, French-speaking, the world's largest cocoa producer) has no "national central bank" of its own: its banking sector is governed by regional institutions. For Asian companies looking to open an account, borrow or find a partner bank locally, understanding who issues licences and who supervises can save a great deal of trouble.

## Who oversees the banks: the BCEAO and the Banking Commission

The Central Bank of West African States (BCEAO, Banque Centrale des États de l'Afrique de l'Ouest) was founded in 1959 and is headquartered in Dakar, Senegal. It is the common central bank of the eight member states of the West African Monetary Union: Benin, Burkina Faso, Côte d'Ivoire, Guinea-Bissau, Mali, Niger, Senegal and Togo. It issues the region's common currency, and the primary objective of its monetary policy is price stability.

Day-to-day banking supervision is the responsibility of the WAMU Banking Commission (Commission Bancaire de l'UMOA). The Commission was set up under a convention signed in Ouagadougou in 1990, is chaired by the Governor of the BCEAO, and has its secretariat in Abidjan. Its powers include granting and withdrawing licences for credit institutions, supervising banks and microfinance institutions, imposing administrative measures and disciplinary sanctions and, where necessary, appointing provisional administrators or liquidators.

In other words, banking licences and supervisory standards are uniform across all eight countries, rather than set by Côte d'Ivoire alone.

## Market size: the largest banking market in the WAMU

According to the Ivorian economic authorities, at the end of 2023 the country had 32 licensed credit institutions: 28 banks (15 international and 13 regional) and 4 financial institutions of a banking nature. That year, total assets of the Ivorian banking system were about XOF 22.19 trillion, or 33.7% of the WAMU total, making it the largest banking market in the union.

Another set of statistics published in 2025, citing BCEAO data, showed that 21 large banks in the union had assets above XOF 1 trillion, and that 11 of them, more than half, were based in Abidjan.

| Indicator (Côte d'Ivoire) | 2020 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|
| Bank branches | 736 | 664 |
| ATMs | 1,133 | 951 |
| Bank accounts | about 4.6 million | about 7.15 million |

The number of branches and ATMs has fallen in recent years while the number of accounts keeps rising, reflecting a shift in how accounts are opened and services delivered.

## Leading commercial banks

By assets, Société Générale's Ivorian subsidiary (Société Générale Côte d'Ivoire) is the regional leader, with assets of about XOF 3.6 trillion in the same statistics. Other banks that regularly rank near the top include NSIA Banque, the Banque Nationale d'Investissement (BNI), BICICI and Bridge Bank Group. Overall, international banking groups, regional banks and domestic banks operate side by side.

## One line for Asian companies

Before choosing a bank, check its status on the list of licensed institutions published by the BCEAO, then compare services for trade finance, foreign exchange and corporate accounts; for questions on account-opening documents, regulation or tax, contact the banks and the authorities directly.
